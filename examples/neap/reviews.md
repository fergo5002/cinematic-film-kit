# Reviews of the narrated example

How Neap was judged and what changed because of it. The reviewer was a fresh agent that saw only
the review packet (contact sheets one frame every 1.25 s, a waveform and a list of what can be
heard, including every narrated line), never the brief or the code. One round only: there was no
critic round for this example.

## The finished film

`neap.mp4`, made by `npm run release -- neap` on macOS (Apple M5) and re-encoded for the repository
at about 2.4 Mbps (the master is 8 Mbps): 21.0 s, 1920x1080, 30 fps, H.264 with AAC, BT.709, the
AI-voice disclosure in its comment tag. `neap.srt` and `neap.vtt` are the captions the master step
wrote beside it. Every release step passed. Film audit: 16 passed, 0 warnings, 0 failed.
Integrated loudness -16 LUFS, loudness range 2.9 LU (the narrated floor is 2.5), true peak
-1.9 dBTP. Narration step: the voice leads the bed by 15.5 dB under speech.

Nobody has listened to it. The voice, its pace and the mix were checked by measurement only.

## Round 1: cold explain-back

On a 22 s cut with a 2.2 s pause after "stops". The viewer recovered the product, all five claims
in order and the sequence of events. The image it kept: "the giant amber 14:52 over the single
frozen peak". It also reported:

| What it said | What changed |
|---|---|
| "I can't tell what Neap is: an app, a website or a service" | the end card says "The tide app for small boats" |
| "Slack water is never explained", and the stop "looks like high tide, not stillness" | the hero reads "Slack water" over "14:52" |
| The 24-hour axis labels are "tiny and faint" | larger, and in the line colour |
| 6.9 to 9.4 s "feels slow", four near-identical frames | the pause after "stops" went from 2.2 s to 1.4 s, and the film from 22 s to 21 s |
| The end card "holds about 3.5 s, a bit long for a bare name" | it now carries what Neap is; the hold is about the same |

Not changed: the faint extra curves for "every harbour" read as unclear without the voice; with it,
they are the line's own illustration. The reviewer's "where I'd stop" was the long hold, which is
now shorter.
