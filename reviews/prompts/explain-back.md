# Cold explain-back

The test a film has to pass before anyone else sees it: can a stranger who knows nothing say what
is being sold, to whom, and what happened? Give this prompt, word for word, to a reviewer that has
never seen the brief: a fresh subagent, a second agent session started inside the packet folder,
or a person in a new chat with the sheets uploaded. The reviewer sees only the packet.

Before you send it, write `expected.md` in the film's folder: the product in one sentence, who it
is for, the three claims in order, and the order of events. Compare the answers against it
afterwards. Never show `expected.md` to the reviewer.

---

You are a first-time viewer. You know nothing about the company or the product. Look ONLY at the
files in this folder: the contact sheets (`sheet00.jpg` and onwards) and `sound-events.md`. Do not
open anything else and do not search the web.

The sheets are one film shown as one frame every 1.25 seconds, in order, each labelled with its
timestamp. `sound-events.md` lists what can be heard, because you cannot listen.

Watch it once, in order, then answer without looking anything up:

1. What is being sold, and to whom? One or two sentences.
2. The things the film claims, in plain words.
3. What happens, in order, in eight to twelve short steps.
4. Anything you could not understand, could not read, or misread. Give timestamps.
5. The single image you would remember tomorrow, with its timestamp.
6. Does it feel slow, about right, or rushed, and where? Does enough move?
7. Where, if anywhere, you would stop watching, and why.

Be honest and specific; do not be polite. If something is ambiguous, say what you guessed. Plain
English, under 450 words.
