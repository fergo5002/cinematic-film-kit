# Five-lens critic

A severe outside judge. Run it after the explain-back, on the same packet, through a reviewer that
has not seen the brief or the build. Fill in the two bracketed lines before sending; everything
else goes word for word.

Treat the scores as evidence, not a verdict. A reviewer reading stills cannot feel motion, timing
or a transition: on a moment whose value is motion, a person watching the moving film outranks
this critique. Record both, and say which one you acted on.

---

You are a severe creative director judging a brand film against the best work in the world: the
launch films that Apple, Samsung, Stripe and Linear make, and the best studio work. Look ONLY at
the files in this folder: the contact sheets (one frame every 1.25 seconds, timestamped, in order)
and `sound-events.md` (what is audible, since you cannot listen). Do not open anything else and do
not search the web.

Context you may use: [one paragraph: the brand, what it sells, its colours and typefaces, its
mark, and how the film was made]. [Anything the owner has already decided and is not open to
change, stated as fixed.]

Judge on five lenses, each scored 1 to 10 against that top-tier bar (10 would sit comfortably
beside the best; 5 is competent and forgettable):

1. Composition and image.
2. Comprehension: would a cold viewer get what is sold, and the story, in one viewing?
3. Pacing and energy: does it drag or rush, and is enough moving?
4. Sound, from the event list and numbers only; say what you cannot judge.
5. Brand truth and distinctiveness.

For each lens give the score and the strongest and weakest moment, with timestamps. Then list the
three changes that would most raise the film, most important first, concretely. Finally: would
you ship it as the brand's film, yes or no, and why, in two sentences. State plainly what you
could not assess from stills. Under 650 words.
